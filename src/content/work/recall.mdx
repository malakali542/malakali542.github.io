## Where Recall was when I joined

Recall takes snapshots of your screen as you work and lets you find anything you've seen on your PC by describing it, with all of the processing on the device. Its announcement in May 2024 drew public privacy and security concerns, and in June it was held back from the Copilot+ PC launch so it could be reworked and previewed with Windows Insiders first ([Windows Experience Blog](https://blogs.windows.com/windowsexperience/2024/06/07/update-on-the-recall-preview-feature-for-copilot-pcs/)). I started at Microsoft AI in July 2024, in the middle of that rework, and Recall went on to reach Insiders in November 2024 and general availability on Copilot+ PCs in April 2025.

I worked on the two parts of Recall that decide whether anyone keeps using it. The first run is where people choose whether to turn it on at all, and search is where they find out whether it was worth it. Both had a gap we could measure.

## Search

Recall is only as good as its search, since a memory you can't search doesn't help anyone. In personal search tools like this one, people find what they wanted on the first try only 10% to 15% of the time. They recover by searching again with better words, and success climbs to 50% to 60% after that second search. Recall matched the category on the first try, but after a second search it only reached about 30%. People who missed weren't finding their way to a better query.

I ran guided search experiments to close that gap. I added suggestions from a small model that complete your query in the search bar while you're still typing, so a vague search turns into a specific one before you press enter. I also added dynamic filters, built from what a search returned, so people could narrow a broad result instead of starting over.

<DemoBlock name="guided-search" caption="Illustration: suggestions while you type, then filters that narrow the results. Not the shipped UI." />

To test ideas like these quickly, I built working prototypes myself with AI prototyping tools and ran my own user studies with them. Participants searched in something that actually responded instead of clicking through static mockups, and ideas reached users before engineering had to build anything.

## The first run

Funnel data showed 30% of first-time users dropping off during onboarding, which made the first run our biggest retention problem. I ran 10+ experiments on that flow and aligned product, UX research, design and data science on one experimentation framework.

The change that worked best was letting people try Recall before committing to it. Turning on a feature that records your screen is a lot to agree to before you've seen what it does, so we let people see it work first. Try-before-you-buy onboarding raised opt-in **15%** and engaged users 5%, and it shaped the roadmap for the onboarding redesign that followed.

## Work PCs, store shelves and the release

Companies decide what runs on their employees' PCs, so Recall needed controls for IT as well as for the person at the keyboard. I shipped the enterprise controls, the policies admins use to decide whether people in their organization can use Recall. On managed PCs it starts off and removed, and even where IT allows it, each person still has to opt in before Recall saves anything ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/client-management/manage-recall)).

A lot of people meet a new Windows feature on a store demo unit long before they use it at home, and that demo has a few seconds to explain itself, so I treated it as a product of its own. I drove Recall's retail launch with engineering, design, retail operations, content and partners, putting on-device AI experiences on millions of in-store devices. The demo reached **30M+ monthly interactions** at a **2.5% sales conversion rate**.

I also led the Responsible AI release. We built input, retrieval and output guardrails into the validation pipeline to block harmful use, and I wrote the risk taxonomies behind classifiers that reached a **98% true positive rate at 0.95 F1**. Getting to launch meant clearing Responsible AI review, the EU's Digital Markets Act and accessibility requirements, with the product under intense public scrutiny the whole time.

## Looking back

Working on search and onboarding at the same time changed how I thought about both. The try-first onboarding worked because people saw Recall find something before we asked them to turn it on, and search was where that first impression either held up or didn't.
