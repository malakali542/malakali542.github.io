## The problem

Recall takes snapshots of your screen and lets you find anything you've seen on your PC by describing it, with all of the processing on the device. It was also one of the most closely watched features Windows has shipped. Its first announcement in 2024 drew public privacy and security concerns, so Recall was reworked, previewed with Windows Insiders in November 2024, and reached general availability on Copilot+ PCs in April 2025.

That left two places where Recall had to earn trust: the first screen a person sees, and the store shelf where many people meet it first. Funnel analysis put a number on the first one. **30% of first-time users dropped off during onboarding**, which made onboarding our biggest retention lever.

## The calls I made

### Let people try it before they commit

I ran **10+ experiments** on the first-run flow with UX research, design and our data science partners. One change stood out: letting people try Recall before committing to it. That try-before-you-buy onboarding raised opt-in **15%** and engaged users **5%**, and it shaped the onboarding redesign roadmap that followed. Along the way I also shipped search, enterprise controls and the first-run experiences themselves.

### Prototype it myself, then test it with users

I ran my own user studies with Recall users. To test ideas before asking engineering to build them, I built working interfaces myself with AI prototyping tools and put them in front of participants. That let me test real interactions instead of static mockups, without waiting on engineering.

### Guide the second search

Recall only works if people can find what they saw, so search is the product. In personal search tools, first-try success usually sits around 10% to 15% and climbs to 50% to 60% once people refine their query and search again. Recall matched the first number, but its success after a re-search only reached about 30%: people who missed on the first try weren't finding their way to a better query. I ran guided search experiments to close that gap:

- **Suggestions as you type.** A small model suggests completions in the search bar while you're still typing, so a vague query becomes a specific one before you hit enter.
- **Dynamic filters.** Filters that adapt to the results let people narrow a broad search instead of starting over.

### Treat the store as a product

Many people meet a new Windows feature on a store shelf before they ever use it at home, so the in-store demo is a product of its own, with a very short attention span. I drove Recall's retail launch across **5+ teams** (engineering, design, retail operations, content and partners), landing on-device AI experiences on millions of in-store devices. The demo reached **30M+ monthly interactions** at a **2.5% sales conversion rate**.

### Build the guardrails into the release

I led the Responsible AI release for Recall. We built input, retrieval and output guardrails into the validation pipeline to block harmful use, and I created the risk taxonomies behind classifiers that reached a **98% true positive rate at 0.95 F1**. Getting to launch also meant clearing Responsible AI review, the EU's Digital Markets Act and accessibility requirements while the product was under intense public scrutiny.

## What I learned

For a privacy-sensitive feature, onboarding is where the architecture becomes something a person can feel. The security work had to be right, and the first-run experience had to make it understandable before anyone was asked to say yes.
