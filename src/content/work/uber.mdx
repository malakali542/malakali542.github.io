## Context

UberX Share brought shared rides back after Uber paused them in 2020. The trade is simple on paper: you might pick up a co-rider heading the same way, and in exchange you get an upfront discount and up to 20% off if you're matched, while arriving no more than 8 minutes later than a regular UberX ([Uber Newsroom](https://www.uber.com/us/en/newsroom/uberx-share/)).

On paper. In the app, a rider who didn't understand that trade would see an unexpected stop or a stranger getting in, and cancel.

## Flows that explain the ride before it surprises you

Some cancellations weren't about price or wait time at all. They came from riders who didn't understand what they had booked. I built flows to cut those cancellations, including the **first-time-user experience for UberX Share in the Rider app**, so the trade is explained before the ride instead of discovered during it.

Alongside that, I ran **A/B experiments on the opt-in funnels** and turned the research and data analysis into feature changes that shipped, lifting shared-ride adoption **4.5%**. I also led the rollout across **4 markets**, coordinating legal, policy and operations so the technical implementation matched each market's regulatory requirements.

## Server-driven UI

Rider flows change constantly, and every change that waits on an app release is slow to test. I **onboarded server-driven UI to our microservice**, so the backend describes what a screen shows and we could change and experiment with flows without shipping a new app version. Once it worked for us, I evangelized it across the shared rides and ride matching org.

## MapStore, 40% faster

MapStore is an internal service other engineering teams use for map data. I redesigned the service interface and the query architecture: **paginated** responses that used to return entire result sets, **rewrote the slowest SQL queries**, and trimmed payloads so the internal tools on top of it **loaded faster**. Average query time dropped **40%** for the teams that depend on it.

## What I learned

The cheapest fix for a confusing product is often an explanation at the right moment, not a new feature. And internal infrastructure is a product too. MapStore's users were engineers on other teams, and they noticed a 40% faster query as much as riders notice a faster pickup.
