## The problem

Loneliness usually isn't about having no friends. It's about the friction between wanting to see people and actually doing it: finding a time that works, deciding what to do, and being the one who asks first. Each step is small, and together they're enough to let a month go by.

## The calls we made

Dots treats your friends as a circle of people orbiting you, and treats free time as something to notice rather than something to schedule.

- **Opportunities, not invitations.** The home screen tells you how many opportunities you have today, meaning windows where people in your circle could get together, with a date, a time and who's in. Accepting or passing takes one tap.
- **A week at a glance.** A strip of days with colored dots under each one hints at who's around, so you can spot a good day before anyone has to ask.
- **Picking what to do.** Once you're in, Dots helps you pick an activity, or you can tell it you've already got a plan.

## My part

I built Dots with [Eric Chen](https://ericdachen.com/design) and our team, Maple Mavericks. My focus was user research, prototyping and motion, from understanding why plans with friends fall through to the prototype in the video above.

## What I learned

Designing for loneliness means designing for the moment before someone reaches out. Dots doesn't add another way to talk to your friends. It removes a few of the reasons not to, and that turned out to be the more interesting product problem.
