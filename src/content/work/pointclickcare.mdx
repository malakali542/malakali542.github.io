## The provider portal

PointClickCare makes cloud software for senior care and post-acute providers, and it describes its EHR as the top senior care EHR in North America ([PointClickCare](https://pointclickcare.com/company/about-us/)). The people using it are clinicians partway through a shift, so a slow page or a form that behaves differently from the last one takes time away from a resident, which makes frontend quality part of the product.

I was a software engineer on the provider portal, one of PointClickCare's web apps for healthcare providers. Two things decided how fast the portal could get better: how its screens were built, and how long every change waited in CI. I worked on both.

## One UI library for every screen

Without a shared library, the same pieces tend to get rebuilt a little differently on every screen, and users feel those differences even when nobody planned them. I built the portal's UI component library, the shared components every screen is assembled from. A button, a form field or a table now looks and behaves the same everywhere, and a new screen starts from parts that are already tested.

## Cutting CI time 30%

Every engineer waits on CI before they know a change is safe to merge, so CI time comes out of everything the team ships. I optimized the CI pipelines and cut their run time **30%**, so every change spent less time waiting before it could merge.
