## The provider portal

PointClickCare makes cloud software for senior care and post-acute providers. It describes itself as the top senior care EHR in North America, with more than 700 million patient visits across its network ([PointClickCare](https://pointclickcare.com/company/about-us/)). I was a software engineer on the provider portal, where providers order new prescriptions, get refills and check the status of their supply.

That work sounds administrative, but it sits close to care. A refill that goes in late can mean a resident waits for a medication, and a supply order nobody can track turns into phone calls during a shift. The people using the portal are care teams partway through their day, so a slow page or a form that behaves differently from the last one takes time away from a resident.

Two things decided how fast the portal, and the apps around it, could get better: how screens were built, and how long every change waited in CI. I worked on both.

## A UI library the whole department used

Without a shared library, the same pieces tend to get rebuilt a little differently in every app, and users feel those differences even when nobody planned them. I built the UI component library, and the whole software engineering department used it. A button, a form field or a table looked and behaved the same across PointClickCare's apps, and every team started new screens from parts that were already tested, so a fix to a shared component reached every screen that used it.

## Cutting CI time 30%

Every engineer waits on CI before they know a change is safe to merge, so CI time comes out of everything the team ships. I optimized the CI pipelines and cut their run time **30%**, so every change spent less time waiting before it could merge.
