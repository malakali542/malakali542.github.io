## The problem

A site decision draws on many records. Planning rules, parcel geometry, terrain and consultant reports from geotechnical, environmental, traffic and civil teams each answer a different part of the same question, and they arrive on different timelines. Someone still has to reconcile them, test the assumptions and decide what to investigate next, and today that person works out of email threads and shared folders.

## The calls we made

### Start with one decision, not all of pre-construction

SwiftZone set out to coordinate everything that happens before construction starts. Our competitor research showed that a general coordination layer was already crowded, and a founder's own acquisition workflow pointed at a sharper first job: research a candidate site against the developer's brief before anyone spends money on diligence. Suelo starts there, and diligence and planning coordination come next.

### Work where developers already work

Suelo runs inside the files and email threads a team already uses, so adopting it doesn't mean adopting another dashboard.

### Keep a person on every decision

Agents prepare the research and draft the follow-up work, and nothing goes out until someone on the team has reviewed it.

### Refuse numbers nobody can source

An early revenue case in our plan turned out to be arithmetic an agent had produced, with no customer behind it, so we gave it no weight. A claim audit then went back through our earlier pitch materials, and anything the evidence didn't establish stayed out of the business case.

## What I built

- **An acquisition agent.** A forwarded candidate site goes through project intake, a review of the project's authorized sources, scenario tools and proposed diligence, and comes out as a decision draft and follow-up emails that wait for a person to review them. Replaying the same request doesn't duplicate work, and for now it runs offline on test fixtures.
- **An assessment that keeps up with the brief.** When a requirement changes, like a minimum site area going from 10 to 12 acres, it re-checks the site, keeps the open questions, and puts the dependent investigation on hold for review.
- **A workbench for the economics.** Rental feasibility, residual land value and sensitivity calculations on sample projects, with every assumption linked to its evidence and review decisions kept separate from the math.
- **The site.** A field-journal design with a Three.js structural study whose floor plates separate as you scroll.

## Where it stands

It's early. Everything so far runs on public and synthetic data, and the next test is whether a developer will pay for a site-research assignment before a real decision. We run Suelo the way I run most things now, with <a href="/ai/">agents</a>: 20 rounds of research, planning, design, building and testing so far, with agent teams applying startup skills for competition, customer discovery, pricing and go-to-market.

## What I've learned

The hardest discipline in agent-assisted research is turning down numbers that look finished. Agents can produce a confident revenue model in minutes, and the job is to ask where every number came from before it shapes a decision.
