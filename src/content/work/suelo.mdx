## The problem

A site decision draws on many records. Planning rules, parcel geometry, terrain and consultant reports from geotechnical, environmental, traffic and civil teams each answer a different part of the same question, and they arrive on different timelines. Someone still has to reconcile them, test the assumptions and prepare the next investigation, and today that person works out of email threads and shared folders.

Suelo started as SwiftZone, an AI coordination layer for pre-construction. Since then the focus has narrowed to the first decision in that chain: whether a candidate site is worth pursuing, and what to investigate next.

## What we're building

Suelo starts with acquisition research. Its agents research candidate sites against a developer's brief, bring together the permitted site records, compare the requirements and flag what's still unresolved. The work product is a candidate comparison, the references behind it, and the questions for the next round of diligence. Diligence and planning coordination come after that.

It works inside the files and email threads a team already uses instead of asking them to adopt another dashboard. Agents prepare the research and the follow-up work, and the team's own professionals review every finding and decide what happens next.

## What I built

- **An acquisition agent.** A forwarded candidate site goes through project intake, a review of the project's authorized sources, scenario tools and proposed diligence, and comes out as a decision draft and follow-up emails that wait for a person to review them. Replaying the same request doesn't duplicate work, and for now it runs offline on test fixtures.
- **An assessment that keeps up with the brief.** When a requirement changes, like a minimum site area going from 10 to 12 acres, it re-checks the site, keeps the open questions, and puts the dependent investigation on hold for review.
- **A workbench for the economics.** Rental feasibility, residual land value and sensitivity calculations on sample projects, with every assumption linked to its evidence and review decisions kept separate from the math.
- **The site.** A field-journal design with a Three.js structural study whose floor plates separate as you scroll.

## How we work

We run Suelo the way I run most things now, with <a href="/ai/">agents</a>: 20 rounds so far of research, planning, design, building and testing, with agent teams applying startup skills for competition, customer discovery, pricing and go-to-market, and a claim audit that strips out anything we can't back up. It's early. Everything so far runs on public and synthetic data while we line up pilots with developers.
